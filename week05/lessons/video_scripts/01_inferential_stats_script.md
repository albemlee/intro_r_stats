## Introduction

Descriptive statistics tells us what's in our sample. But in research, we usually care about something bigger — the population our sample came from. This lesson introduces inferential statistics: the tools we use to move honestly from what we measured to what we believe is true.

---

## [SLIDE: Inferential Stats]

Here's the core challenge. We can never measure an entire population. We take a sample, measure it, and then try to draw conclusions about the broader group.

The problem is that any sample comes with uncertainty. A different random sample from the same population would give slightly different results. So how confident can we be that our sample's findings reflect something real about the population?

That's exactly the question inferential statistics is designed to answer.

---

## [SLIDE: The Uncertainty Around Sample Statistics]

Let's revisit the penguin example. Our sample of 344 penguins shows that Chinstrap penguins have longer bills than Adelie penguins. But our sample is finite. A different sample might show a slightly different gap. Some samples might even show no gap at all, just by chance.

Inferential statistics is how we account for that uncertainty — and how we communicate it honestly.

---

## [SLIDE: Inferential Statistics Defined]

**Inferential statistics** uses sample data to make inferences about a population or underlying mechanism, while being explicit about the uncertainty involved.

The three core tools are:

A **point estimate** — your single best guess at the population value, based on your sample.

A **standard error** — a measure of how much your estimate might vary if you repeated the study with a different sample.

A **confidence interval** — a range of values that likely contains the true population value.

These three work together. Instead of just reporting a sample mean, you report: "Here's our best guess, here's how uncertain we are, and here's the plausible range."

---

## [SLIDE: Knowledge Check]

A study measures average systolic blood pressure in 500 patients and finds a mean of 128 mmHg. Which of these is an inferential statement?

a) "The mean systolic BP in our sample is 128 mmHg."  
b) "The average systolic BP in the population is approximately 128 mmHg."

Statement b is inferential — it's making a claim about the population. Statement a is purely descriptive — it describes the sample. Both sentences look similar on the surface, but they mean very different things scientifically.
